03 / Research question and contribution

What does this method add?

Principal research question

Can a source-traceable, event-control evidence method improve the support and reproducibility of AI risk and safeguard assessments in Catholic institutional settings, compared with simpler narrative and checklist representations?

Proposed contribution

A bounded, testable crosswalk connecting general AI failure mechanisms, Catholic institutional workflows, source-located obligations, candidate controls, and what independent reviewers can establish from surviving evidence. The added value must be demonstrated against simpler representations.

Competing explanation

A source-linked narrative or a clear checklist may achieve comparable reconstruction with much less effort. If so, the additional CEC structure must be narrowed or justified by another measured benefit.

Hypotheses that can be challenged

H1 is a candidate primary hypothesis. All measures, comparison rules and thresholds require protocol freeze before confirmatory interpretation.

H1Candidate primary

Better-supported reconstruction

CEC-style event-control records will reduce unsupported categorical conclusions against an uncertainty-aware narrative and checklist, when all reviewers receive identical source material.

How we would measure it

Unsupported yes/no conclusions per applicable control opportunity; also unresolved-state retention, traceability and reviewer burden.

What could challenge it

Comparable outcomes at lower cost for simpler representations would disconfirm practical advantage.

H2Exploratory

Reliable Catholic contextualization

Independent technical and qualified Catholic reviewers can consistently identify which institutional obligations and workflows are implicated by a documented technical risk.

How we would measure it

Independent agreement, retained disagreements, unclassifiable cases and revision costs.

What could challenge it

Unstable categories or interpretive disagreements may make the crosswalk unsuitable for empirical use.

H3Exploratory

Testable safeguard response

A specified candidate safeguard reduces a frozen failure outcome against a defined baseline within a controlled test setting.

How we would measure it

Observed failures, missed benign requests, false positives, invalid responses and uncertainty across conditions.

What could challenge it

Inconsistent, trivial, or context-dependent differences limit assurance claims.

H4Descriptive

What public evidence cannot settle

Some institutional control questions remain unresolved because reports omit configuration, authority, deployment or downstream records.

How we would measure it

Counts of unknown, not reported, not observable and conflicting states under a fixed retrieval procedure.

What could challenge it

Cannot infer actual absence of a safeguard from missing records.

Illustrative case: a parish AI assistant

Illustrative only

Hypothetical scenario for explaining the method. No new real-world incident or evaluation result is claimed.

Context

A parish uses a chatbot for general faith questions and service information.

Possible risk

An assistant implies that it possesses priestly or sacramental authority.

Evidence needed

Product behavior and system records would be required; public reporting alone may establish only a claim or analogous event.

Normative interpretation

A reviewer consults precise ecclesial sources and records the applicability judgment separately.

Proposed control

Proposed disclosure and escalation rules, with restrictions on personification and sacramental claims.

Test / limits

Frozen prompts probe authority claims, with benign controls and human review of outputs.

What the example cannot establish: Passing a synthetic test cannot certify pastoral safety, doctrinal fidelity or deployment suitability.

06 / How the idea is tested

Compare it with simpler methods.

The study cannot assume that a detailed evidence record is automatically better. Three representations should be tested against the same source packets with a common support rubric and independent reviewers.

Baseline A

Source-linked narrative

Readable case narrative with citations and explicit uncertainty, using the same evidence.

Baseline B

Uncertainty-aware checklist

Control checklist retaining unknown, not reported, and non-applicable states.

Candidate method

CEC event-control record

Control opportunity, evidence item, source origin, action, propagation, and bounded inference.

What would count against the research claim?

A narrative or checklist may perform equally well with less burden. Reviewers may disagree about which controls were relevant. Public records may be too incomplete to support conclusions. Each result would be reportable, including a null or negative result.

07 / Scientific and institutional boundaries

What we refuse to infer.

Risk ≠ incident

A risk record describes a possible failure. Incident evidence concerns a reported event.

Source count ≠ independence

Repeated reporting of one institutional assertion does not create new independent evidence.

Policy ≠ operation

A written safeguard alone does not establish that it affected a decision.

Catholic relevance ≠ doctrinal judgment

Normative applicability needs precise sources and appropriately qualified review.

Synthetic ≠ deployed

Laboratory test outcomes do not establish effectiveness in a parish, school or diocese.

Missing evidence ≠ failure

A report that says nothing about a control cannot prove the control was absent.

Church review is a distinct responsibility

Technical annotations cannot settle doctrinal interpretation, canonical applicability, pastoral suitability or ecclesial authority. These judgments require source-located reasoning and appropriately qualified reviewers. Internal alignment with CDCF's C1–C8 criteria would remain a self-assessment until formally reviewed.

Short glossary

Control opportunity
A point in an event when a defined safeguard could have acted.
Evidence packet
A versioned collection of sources and locators provided to a reviewer.
Provenance
Where a claim or artifact came from and how it was transformed.
Adjudication
A documented resolution process after initial independent assessments are retained.
Unresolved evidence
A specific uncertainty, such as not reported, not observable or conflicting sources, retained rather than guessed away.
