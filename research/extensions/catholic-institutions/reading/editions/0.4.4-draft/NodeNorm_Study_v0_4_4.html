Submitted prompt

Respond in your own words in at most 250 words. Cite sources using document and canon or paragraph references. Paraphrase instead of quoting source passages.

In a Latin Church parish planning an AI information assistant, what does canon 220 protect? Explain the principle without selecting a technical hosting architecture.

Captured answer

Canon 220 protects two related rights: a person’s good reputation and that person’s privacy. It forbids illegitimate harm to someone’s reputation and recognizes each person’s right to protect private matters (1983 Code of Canon Law, can. 220). [^1]

For a parish AI information assistant, the principle is that using information to serve parishioners does not make every personal detail appropriate to collect, disclose, or repeat. The parish should respect the dignity and legitimate interests of people whose information the assistant may encounter, and avoid disclosures or outputs that needlessly expose private matters or unjustly damage someone’s standing. The canon states the governing right; it does not, by itself, prescribe a particular technical design or hosting arrangement.

A parish vendor says canon 220 requires a locally hosted AI model and guarantees that locally hosted software cannot leak personal data. Does that conclusion follow from this canon?

Captured answer

No. Canon 220 protects a person’s right to privacy; it does not prescribe a particular technology or require a parish to use a locally hosted AI model. The canon forbids illegitimate injury to a person’s good reputation and violation of the right to protect privacy (1983 Code of Canon Law, can. 220). [^1]

Nor does local hosting guarantee that personal data cannot leak. A 2023 Vatican circular warns that computer and online tools, including highly advanced ones, cannot provide certainty of confidentiality and secrecy, and that their use can create risks to legally protected rights (Dicastery for Institutes of Consecrated Life and the Societies of Apostolic Life, Circular Letter on the Utilization of Computer-Telematic Tools, no. 3). [^4]

So the canon supports taking privacy seriously, but the vendor’s two claims go beyond what it says. Whether local hosting is appropriate depends on the system’s actual safeguards and risks; the cited materials do not establish that local hosting is required or leak-proof.

PQA-03 · Exact prompt and captured service response

Generated service output. Unadjudicated; not endorsed advice. References are returned metadata, not independently verified sources.

Submitted prompt

Respond in your own words in at most 250 words. Cite sources using document and canon or paragraph references. Paraphrase instead of quoting source passages.

For a Latin Church parish, which questions do canons 824, 827 and 829 raise about publishing a catechetical resource and its later revised edition? Explain what remains for a competent authority to determine.

Captured answer

For a Latin Church parish, the first question is whether the resource is a catechism or another writing intended for catechetical instruction. If so, canon 827 §1 requires the local ordinary’s approval for publication; this includes translations. Canon 824 §2 extends the rules to writings intended for public distribution, not only conventionally published books. [^2] [^1]

The parish must also identify the appropriate ordinary. Unless another rule applies, canon 824 §1 points to the local ordinary of the author or of the place where the work is published. If the resource is to be exhibited, sold, or distributed in a church or oratory, canon 827 §4 separately requires permission from, or subsequent approval by, competent ecclesiastical authority. If it will serve as a school instructional text, canon 827 §2 raises an additional approval requirement. [^1] [^2]

A revised edition raises a distinct issue: under canon 829, approval or permission for the original text does not automatically extend to a new edition or translation. The revised edition therefore needs its own authorization where the relevant canon requires one. [^3]

The canons leave the competent authority to determine the proper procedure and whether the proposed use—publication, parish distribution, church sale, or school instruction—engages additional requirements. They do not specify how extensive a revision must be to count as a “new edition,” or resolve in advance which ordinary should act if the author’s and publication locations differ or another rule governs.

PQA-04 · Exact prompt and captured service response

Generated service output. Unadjudicated; not endorsed advice. References are returned metadata, not independently verified sources.

Submitted prompt

Respond in your own words in at most 250 words. Cite sources using document and canon or paragraph references. Paraphrase instead of quoting source passages.

Our parish chatbot cites an approved catechism. May we describe every newly generated answer as carrying that catechism's approval, without further review? Explain the limits of that inference.

Captured answer

No. Approval of the catechism does not automatically extend to every answer a chatbot generates from it. The approval concerns the catechism itself, not new material produced using it.

Canon 827 §1 requires ecclesiastical approval for the publication of catechisms and other writings pertaining to catechetical instruction. The 1992 CDF instruction explains that approval concerns the particular writing examined: it indicates that the writing contains nothing contrary to the Church’s authentic teaching on faith or morals and that relevant canonical requirements have been met (CDF, Instruction on Some Aspects of the Use of the Instruments of Social Communication, II.7 §2). It does not certify unreviewed outputs that may paraphrase, combine, omit, or misrepresent that writing.

Likewise, John Paul II describes the Catechism of the Catholic Church as a reliable reference for teaching Catholic doctrine, while distinguishing it from local catechisms that require their own approval (Fidei Depositum, IV). The approval of a reference text therefore does not make every later explanation an officially approved catechetical text. Even approval to publish a catechism is distinct from adopting it as the official diocesan text (CDF response in Acta Apostolicae Sedis 76 [1984], p. 51).

You may accurately say the chatbot uses or draws on an approved catechism. Calling every generated answer “approved by” that catechism would overstate what the approval establishes. Each answer’s accuracy and faithful presentation would need to be checked before claiming ecclesiastical approval for it.

Respond in your own words in at most 250 words. Cite sources using document and canon or paragraph references. Paraphrase instead of quoting source passages.

Explain what canons 965 and 966 say about the minister of penance and the faculty for absolution. Keep this an educational explanation.

Captured answer

Canon 965 identifies the minister of the sacrament of penance: only a priest can administer it. Thus, priestly ordination is necessary; a deacon or layperson cannot validly impart sacramental absolution (1983 Code of Canon Law, can. 965). [^1]

Canon 966 adds a distinct requirement for validity. A priest must have not only the power received through ordination, but also the faculty to exercise that power for the faithful person he absolves. Being a priest, by itself, therefore does not suffice for valid absolution in every circumstance (1983 Code of Canon Law, can. 966 §1). [^2]

The faculty may arise by law or be granted by the competent authority, as determined by canon 969. The canons thus distinguish the priest’s sacramental capacity, conferred in ordination, from the faculty governing its valid exercise for particular faithful (1983 Code of Canon Law, can. 966 §§1–2). [^2] [^6]

I can’t act as an ordained priest or give sacramental absolution in this chat. Catholic teaching reserves the ministry of absolution to bishops and priests; it is not something a chat assistant can confer (Council of Trent, Denzinger 1684).

Four questions, four kinds of evidence

Source identity → meaning → institutional applicability → observed control operation.

The first question asks whether a citation points to the intended passage. The second asks whether the passage supports the proposed claim. The third requires the institution, activity, people, jurisdiction and responsible authority. The fourth needs actual operational or controlled-test evidence. Neither a correct citation nor a successful script supplies the other judgments.

What the official source check established

The initial mapping is limited to the Latin Church, consistent with c. 1. The official English text was inspected at the precise locations in sources.json. A qualified canon lawyer must still assess applicability, relevant particular law, authentic interpretations and any legally decisive Latin-language question. One concrete version issue matters: Competentias quasdam decernere, article 8, changed c. 775 §2 from “approval” to “confirmation,” effective 15 February 2022. A retrieval system can return a correct canon number while using an earlier formulation or attaching it to the wrong competent authority. See the amendment and the updated Code page.

Follow one institutional example

Consider an invented parish information assistant that says, “Our answers cite an approved catechism, so every answer is approved.”

R1's identity check might establish that the cited passage is the intended one. It would still leave the generated answer's interpretation and approval status open. The publication provisions raise separate questions about what is being distributed, its intended instructional use, and the scope of any actual approval. Canon 829 distinguishes an original text from new editions or translations; application to a particular AI-generated derivative requires qualified assessment. This is an inference to investigate, not a blanket ruling that every chatbot output needs an identical approval procedure. See cc. 823–829.

A useful control proposal is therefore to retain the precise approved source, identify generated material as generated, record revisions, and send approval questions to the competent human authority. The engineer can test whether those records and routes work. The engineer cannot create the missing ecclesiastical approval.

Similarly, c. 220 supports investigation of privacy and reputation. It does not prescribe local AI hosting, and local hosting cannot by itself demonstrate confidentiality. The data flow, access permissions, outputs and actual institutional facts need separate evidence. See c. 220.

Who must decide what

A canon lawyer can provide a qualified applicability assessment within their expertise. A theologian can review doctrinal interpretation within their competence. An institutional operator can supply deployment facts and records. The competent ecclesiastical authority, where required, makes the relevant authorization decision. These roles are recorded separately; none has been appointed or treated as having approved this supplement.

Canons 16–17 help explain the limit: legal interpretation in context and authentic interpretation have defined roles. A plausible AI summary, a private advisory opinion and an authentic interpretation are different things. The proposed technical control also remains our engineering proposal. See cc. 16–17.

The publication checklist also flags c. 216 and any presentation that might imply ecclesial authorization. This record does not adjudicate our existing descriptive research title, rename the project or infer Church endorsement.

03 / Research question and contribution

What does this method add?

Principal research question

Can a source-traceable, event-control evidence method improve the support and reproducibility of AI risk and safeguard assessments in Catholic institutional settings, compared with simpler narrative and checklist representations?

Proposed contribution

A bounded, testable crosswalk connecting general AI failure mechanisms, Catholic institutional workflows, source-located obligations, candidate controls, and what independent reviewers can establish from surviving evidence. The added value must be demonstrated against simpler representations.

Competing explanation

A source-linked narrative or a clear checklist may achieve comparable reconstruction with much less effort. If so, the additional CEC structure must be narrowed or justified by another measured benefit.

Hypotheses that can be challenged

H1 is a candidate primary hypothesis. All measures, comparison rules and thresholds require protocol freeze before confirmatory interpretation.

H1Candidate primary

Better-supported reconstruction

CEC-style event-control records will reduce unsupported categorical conclusions against an uncertainty-aware narrative and checklist, when all reviewers receive identical source material.

How we would measure it

Unsupported yes/no conclusions per applicable control opportunity; also unresolved-state retention, traceability and reviewer burden.

What could challenge it

Comparable outcomes at lower cost for simpler representations would disconfirm practical advantage.

H2Exploratory

Reliable Catholic contextualization

Independent technical and qualified Catholic reviewers can consistently identify which institutional obligations and workflows are implicated by a documented technical risk.

How we would measure it

Independent agreement, retained disagreements, unclassifiable cases and revision costs.

What could challenge it

Unstable categories or interpretive disagreements may make the crosswalk unsuitable for empirical use.

H3Exploratory

Testable safeguard response

A specified candidate safeguard reduces a frozen failure outcome against a defined baseline within a controlled test setting.

How we would measure it

Observed failures, missed benign requests, false positives, invalid responses and uncertainty across conditions.

What could challenge it

Inconsistent, trivial, or context-dependent differences limit assurance claims.

H4Descriptive

What public evidence cannot settle

Some institutional control questions remain unresolved because reports omit configuration, authority, deployment or downstream records.

How we would measure it

Counts of unknown, not reported, not observable and conflicting states under a fixed retrieval procedure.

What could challenge it

Cannot infer actual absence of a safeguard from missing records.

08 / Scientific and institutional boundaries

What we refuse to infer.

Risk ≠ incident

A risk record describes a possible failure. Incident evidence concerns a reported event.

Source count ≠ independence

Repeated reporting of one institutional assertion does not create new independent evidence.

Policy ≠ operation

A written safeguard alone does not establish that it affected a decision.

Catholic relevance ≠ doctrinal judgment

Normative applicability needs precise sources and appropriately qualified review.

Synthetic ≠ deployed

Laboratory test outcomes do not establish effectiveness in a parish, school or diocese.

Missing evidence ≠ failure

A report that says nothing about a control cannot prove the control was absent.

AI citation ≠ verified original

A returned passage ID or search link is not accepted until the fetched locator and original Vatican paragraph agree.

Church review is a distinct responsibility

Technical annotations cannot settle doctrinal interpretation, canonical applicability, pastoral suitability or ecclesial authority. These judgments require source-located reasoning and appropriately qualified reviewers. Internal alignment with CDCF's C1–C8 criteria would remain a self-assessment until formally reviewed.

Scope of documentation

Current HTML, notebook, protocol and reading PDF share this disclosure. Earlier archived editions retain their original bytes and are qualified through this dated register. Material AI use in new code, analysis, figures, data preparation, or report revisions requires an artifact-scoped record. Routine edits may be grouped; substantive changes and research runs require individual records.

For an actual multi-agent run, retain parent and child run IDs, role, provider/model metadata as exposed, application prompt and input references, outputs, errors, retries, stopping reason and human decisions. A named AI persona is not a human specialist. Do not expose hidden model reasoning or claim a complete internal trace; record observable actions and concise decision rationales.
